Language models as the weight solver: why the predictions are wrong

Analysis for the meeting of 19 September 2026. Continues the language-model section of the 10 September report (9-10-update.md §6). That report established how much error each arm carries. This one asks what kind of error it is: of every prediction that missed, what made it miss. Nothing in the earlier report is revised. Scripts and tables: §7.

1. What is being compared

Method. Five hosted language models used as the weight solver on the band-space synthetic historical control design: given one site's own band means for nine consecutive months, predict the tenth. The withheld month is P10 (August 2024); all ten months are pre-hurricane and nothing in this validation is treated. Twenty-seven target cells, twelve on Sentinel-1 and fifteen on Sentinel-2.

Two prompts, carrying the same evidence and the same one-line answer format, neither containing any word for hurricane, landslide or damage.

  • The direct prompt: a band guide, the site's nine monthly rows, the instruction to predict the next month.
  • The step-by-step prompt: the same, plus the band-space pipeline's procedure in prose — smooth each month against its neighbours, treat equal-length earlier stretches as candidate controls, score each against the most recent stretch, add candidates while each improves the match, weight them non-negatively to sum to one, apply those weights to the values following each stretch. The wording was validated clause by clause against the pipeline's notebook before any call was made.

Metric. The band-space pipeline's own held-out measure, which its notebook calls the joint validation NMSE: per band the squared error at the withheld month divided by the variance of that band across P01–P09, averaged over bands. Lower is better. That variance is the pipeline's own train_var, taken over the periods its fit is allowed to see. Scored this way the re-run reproduces its twenty-seven published per-site values to 4.2 × 10⁻⁷ and its sensor means of 0.4925 and 0.7867, so every figure below is directly comparable with its published numbers. An earlier draft of this report used the variance over P01–P10, which is not that pipeline's denominator; every number here has been recomputed.

Provenance. Neither gateway is reproducible — the research gateway accepts a temperature of zero and ignores it, and the gateway serving the GPT-5 family rejects the parameter outright. The direct prompt has two runs per open-weight model and two for GPT-5.5; the step-by-step prompt has one of each. GPT-5.5 was reached two ways and the name resolves to two different snapshots, dated 24 and 23 April 2026; both are reported, and every effect below appears on both.

3. Why the predictions are wrong

Seven causes, separated so that each can be judged on its own evidence. They are not equally large: §3.2 and §3.3 account for most of the error on Sentinel-1, §3.5 for much of the Sentinel-2 mean, and §3.7 is small and included because it is a real defect that the headline metric hides.

3.1 No answer was produced at all

Method. Calls that never returned a parsable line, counted by the reason the gateway reported.

GLM-5.3 direct 12 12 output budget spent on reasoning
GLM-5.3 step-by-step 12 15 output budget spent on reasoning
Kimi-K3 step-by-step 1 6 + 1 budget spent; one degenerate loop
DeepSeek-V4-Flash either all all the model name is not served

GLM-5.3 does not terminate: its stored traces run past 21,000 characters without reaching an answer line, on a task where gpt-oss-120b finishes in 156 tokens. This is the model, not the budget. Kimi-K3's losses concentrate on Sentinel-2, where eight bands make the arithmetic longest. These failures are not accuracy problems and do not appear in any mean; they are a separate, prior failure to produce output at all.

On Sentinel-1, 33 of 36 band-months reversed; only 3 continued. On Sentinel-2, 66 of 120 reversed. The monthly series is close to mean-reverting at this horizon, and every model — and the band-space pipeline, and the three-month mean — is some form of forward projection of recent behaviour. Such a predictor is wrong almost every time by construction, and the error is concentrated there by a factor of three to nine.

This is not a defect of any model. It is the property of the data that sets the floor under all of these numbers, and it is the single largest reason the predictions are wrong.

Every model, every prompt, both sensors predicts too high. The bias is 62–81 % of the spread on Sentinel-1, so a large share of that error is a constant offset rather than random miss. On Sentinel-1 the bands are backscatter in decibels, so "too high" means the predicted return is too strong; the withheld August month sits lower than every model expects.

It is not a model fault. The same signed error was computed for the no-model rules, which have no reasoning to blame:

Sentinel-1 +0.436 +0.279 +0.485 −0.046
Sentinel-2 +0.367 +0.347 +0.342 +0.282

Every rule that looks at recent months is too high by about as much as the models are. Only the site's own nine-month mean escapes it on Sentinel-1, and only because it ignores where the series recently was.

In plain units, what happened is that August was a low month. Averaged over the twelve Sentinel-1 sites:

July (P09) mean of P07–P09 August, actual
VV −8.92 dB −9.07 dB −9.31 dB
VH −15.20 dB −15.36 dB −15.66 dB

August came in 0.38 dB below July on VV, and below July at all twelve sites. A model that predicted "about July's level" was therefore too high, and so was repeating July exactly (too high by 0.38 dB) and so was averaging the last three months (too high by 0.24 dB).

So this is not the models leaning high. It is August sitting below everything that came before it, and every method that reads the recent months inherits the miss. No prompt removes it. Subtracting a constant would remove it, but the constant could only be chosen by looking at the answer.

3.4 The step-by-step procedure is degenerate: executed correctly, it returns the last month

Checked a second, independent way — against the carry-forward reference scored separately — the step-by-step arm sits 0.005–0.028 from it on Sentinel-2 with twelve or thirteen of fifteen sites within 0.01, where the direct arm sits 0.25–0.83 away. The two checks agree, including on where the collapse is only partial: Sentinel-1 gpt-oss-120b resists on both.

The clearest single piece of evidence is Sentinel-2 site 0003, where three models from three organisations, on two gateways, return the same eight numbers to three decimals — and those numbers are row P09. They are not producing different answers that happen to score alike; they are all returning the same row.

Two different routes reach that one answer, and they must not be conflated. The traces were read for whether the five windows are actually worked with:

model traces naming all five windows stating the most recent window alone was selected
Kimi-K3 16 / 27 (26 of 27 name at least one) 24 / 27
GLM-5.3 23 / 27 20 / 27
gpt-oss-120b 4 / 27 (6 name any) 1 / 27
GPT-5.5 0 / 25 (7 name any) 3 / 25

Route one — the procedure executed correctly. Kimi-K3 and GLM-5.3 do the block arithmetic and reach the most recent window on their own. Kimi-K3, closing a trace:

"…try each window alone against smoothed P06–P09 and keep best; then adding windows refitting weights; add if improves fit… W5-only is plausible.… Use given VV-VH 6.00 not recompute 5.98. Raw P09 VV-VH given 6.00."

That is the procedure followed faithfully, including the stopping rule. And the most recent window is P05–P08, whose forward month is P09 — so a correct execution returns the previous month.

The estimator is degenerate on this data, and that is not an artifact of a vague threshold. The reference implementation uses an improvement threshold of 1 × 10⁻⁴ — it accepts almost any gain at all — and still keeps one window at 26 of 27 sites (the most recent; the twenty-seventh keeps two). Loosening the rule cannot produce a blend, because there is nothing to add that improves the pre-period fit. The procedure, correctly executed with the loosest admissible stopping rule, is P09 carried forward.

Route two — the procedure abandoned. gpt-oss-120b barely engages with the windows at all (six traces mention one, none carry per-window numbers) and says why:

"Probably smoothing via 3-month moving average?… I'll just take raw as smoothed (due time). Then windows 4-month averages? Probably too complex. I'll guess slight increase trend…"

For GPT-5.5 the route cannot be determined: only summaries are available, and a summarizer may drop the arithmetic whether or not it happened.

The distinction matters for what can be fixed. If the models were failing to execute, a more precise instruction would help. Two of the four are executing correctly, and the procedure's own answer is the last observed month — so a clearer instruction would move gpt-oss-120b and GPT-5.5 towards P09, not away from it. This is why the step-by-step arm's sign differs by sensor: carrying P09 forward scores 0.492 on Sentinel-1 and 0.789 on Sentinel-2, while the direct prompt scores better than that on Sentinel-1 and worse on Sentinel-2.

3.5 The input month is contaminated

At site 0004 the last observed month carries residual cloud: blue reflectance nearly doubles against both neighbours (0.045 → 0.086) and the greenness index falls sharply (0.761 → 0.621). The withheld month returns to the seasonal track (0.054, 0.725).

Every method anchored on the last month is misled, the band-space pipeline included, and the models compound it by projecting the contaminated direction further. Averaging over three months dilutes the bad month and scores an order of magnitude better than any of them. This is a data-quality failure before it is a prediction failure, and it is not addressable by prompting: the information needed is not in the nine rows the model is given.

3.6 The metric concentrates the score in the flattest bands

Method. Share of the Sentinel-2 squared error carried by each band, against each band's denominator.

band B2 B3 B4 B8 B11 B12 NDVI NDWI
share of total error, GPT-5.5 direct 13.1 % 8.8 % 22.0 % 1.3 % 22.1 % 17.2 % 5.3 % 10.3 %
share of total error, gpt-oss direct 24.2 % 13.8 % 21.2 % 2.7 % 18.3 % 10.4 % 4.5 % 4.9 %
the band's own SD (mean over sites) 0.018 0.021 0.020 0.138 0.031 0.029 0.183 0.097

The near-infrared band carries 1–3 % of the error despite being the largest band and the one that moves most across the season. The reason is the denominator: its standard deviation is roughly seven times that of blue, so its variance is around forty-eight times larger, and the same proportional miss scores far lower there than on a flat band. Red, blue and the shortwave-infrared pair — the quiet bands — own three quarters of the score.

This is a property of the metric rather than of the models, and it is stated here because it governs how every other number in this document should be read: a method is rewarded for getting the flat bands right, not the informative one. It is the pipeline's own metric and has not been changed.

3.7 Imposing algebra the data does not obey

The Sentinel-1 difference band is an exact identity. The observed rows satisfy it to 0.0007 decibels, which is rounding. Predictions:

The Sentinel-2 indices are the opposite case. They are formed per day and each band is composited independently, so the observed data does not satisfy the textbook formulas: the real rows depart from them by 0.009–0.012, and the withheld month by 0.004.

GPT-5.5, 23 April direct 0.0004 0.0039

GPT-5.5 computes its indices from its own predicted bands exactly, making its answer roughly ten times more internally consistent than the data it is predicting. The magnitude is small — a few thousandths of an index unit, against a standard deviation of 0.18 for greenness — so this is a minor contributor, listed because it is a genuine defect that the joint metric cannot show.

6. What the causes imply

Ordered by how much error each would remove, not by how easy it is.

  1. Reversal (§3.2) sets the floor and no prompt reaches it. Any predictor that projects recent behaviour is wrong on the 33 of 36 Sentinel-1 band-months that reverse. What helps is not committing to a direction — which is exactly why the three-month mean leads on both sensors (0.265 and 0.541) while every model and the band-space pipeline sit above it.
  2. The upward bias (§3.3) is not a model fault and not a prompt target. The no-model rules carry the same offset (+0.28 to +0.49), so it belongs to the withheld month. The one unbiased predictor is the site's own nine-month mean on Sentinel-1 (−0.046), which ignores the recent level entirely — the same lesson as §3.2.
  3. Contamination (§3.5) is a data problem. The information needed to discount a clouded month is not in the nine rows given to the model. Passing a quality flag, or a cloud-fraction column, would be a change to the evidence rather than to the prompt.
  4. The step-by-step prompt (§3.4) cannot be fixed by clearer wording. Two of the four models execute it correctly and reach the most recent window on their own; the procedure's own answer is the previous month. A more precise instruction would move the other two towards that answer, not away from it. Its apparent Sentinel-2 gain is the degenerate answer scoring well on that sensor, not better reasoning. Changing the estimator — the window set, the stopping rule — would change the result; changing the prose will not.
  5. Read every number here through §3.6. The metric rewards the flat bands; the near-infrared band, which carries the seasonal signal, contributes 1–3 % of the score.
