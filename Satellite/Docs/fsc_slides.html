1 · Input representation

The TerraMind embedding: where 5 × 14 × 14 comes from

Site≈ 1 km × 1 km of ground, one satellite chip per sensor per 14-day period
→
Resized chip224 × 224 pixels, cut into 16 × 16-pixel patches
→
14 × 14 = 196 parcelseach patch ≈ 72 m × 72 m of ground
→
5 latents per parcelxp ∈ ℝ⁵
→
980 values / chip196 × 5: five 14 × 14 maps stacked

What the 5 channels are

  • Learned internal features of TerraMind, not the original bands (not Red, NIR, VV, or VH).
  • A chip is a collection of 196 learned local descriptors {x1, …, x196}; the 5 numbers carry no real-world meaning on their own.
  • Measured meaning (clean chips): the 5 values predict spectral indices at the same parcel: within-chip R² NDVI 0.71, NDWI 0.65, NDBI 0.80.

The codebook (TerraMind paper, arXiv:2504.11171)

  • “Modality-specific tokenizers”: ViT encoder + patched UNet decoder; one latent per 16 × 16 patch, discretized with finite-scalar quantization (FSQ).
  • “The latent dimension was set to 5”, FSQ levels 8-8-8-6-5 → 8·8·8·6·5 = 15,360 ≈ 16K codewords.
  • FSQ chosen for training stability (fixed, not learned, codebook); compression 250–3000×.
  • We use the frozen pretrained S2-L2A and S1-GRD tokenizers.
1 · The failure mode

Why not parcel-to-parcel comparison

The problem is not that 980 dimensions are too many. Treating the latent as one long vector makes every distance and every SC fit position-matched: parcel (3, 7) of the treated site is compared with parcel (3, 7) of a donor, which is a different piece of ground at a different geographic site. A forest patch in the upper-left corner of one chip is compared against a road or a river in another.

Tokens are local descriptors

On clean chips the 5 values describe that parcel’s ground (within-chip R² 0.65–0.80). So a position-matched distance compares unrelated objects, such as one site’s parking lot and another site’s forest.

The in-sample fit was an illusion

Shuffling donor parcels before fitting moves Sentinel-2 train error from 0.820 to 1.114: the apparent fit was alignment overfitting, and it does not survive the holdout.

The fix

Pool within the chip first: compare what is in the chip (distributions, co-activation structure), not where it sits. This pooled object is exactly what FSC is built for.

Our 100-dimensional outcome is the quantile kind: 5 channels × 20 quantiles = 100 (grid 0.01…0.99, panel_repr.GRIDS_Q).

Why the distinction matters

The FSC paper’s Wasserstein result is stated on quantile functions:

W22(μ, ν) = ∫01  Fμ−1(u) − Fν−1(u) 2 du

Euclidean distance between bin counts is not automatically W2; between quantile vectors it is, per channel. Averaging the donors’ quantile vectors with the synthetic-control weights gives a Wasserstein barycenter.

The synthetic-control weights only combine the donors’ quantile vectors into the counterfactual; no site’s own representation is changed, and the combination is again a valid quantile function, matching the FSC paper’s mortality application.

2 · Pooled representations — 2 of 2

The Gram matrix: what fires together at the same parcel

G = (1/196) 196Σp=1 xpxp⊤   ∈ ℝ5×5, symmetric → 15 numbers (upper triangle incl. diagonal)
  • Diagonal Gcc = mean of xpc2 — the average “energy” of channel c over the chip.
  • Off-diagonal Gcd = mean of xpcxpd — do channels c and d light up on the same parcels? High-together → large G12; high-where-the-other-is-low → small or negative.
  • This keeps cross-channel structure that the 5 separate marginal distributions cannot retain.
G11G12G13G14G15 G12G22G23G24G25 G13G23G33G34G35 G14G24G34G44G45 G15G25G35G45G55

Implementation: A @ A.T / 196, vectorized column-major to match the authors’ covvec.

Technical note: this is an uncentered second-moment matrix; a covariance would first subtract each channel’s mean. Both are positive-semidefinite (PSD), and PSD matrices form a convex cone, so an average under the synthetic-control weights is itself a valid matrix (the FSC paper’s covariance application).

What the quantile arm misses

A marsh and a forest beside a lake can have identical per-channel distributions but different co-activation — the Gram separates them.

What the Gram misses

A half-destroyed site and a uniformly lightly-hit site can share second moments but differ in the full distribution — the quantiles separate them. Combined arm = both blocks, 115-d.

The three comparisons — and why three

A small error alone proves nothing; each comparison rules out one trivial explanation of it.

C1 — did the fit merely memorize? Test error ≤ 1.5 × training error. This is the collaborator’s notebook-14 flag — the one check both pipelines share.
C2 (ours) — prediction error at the test period < error of predicting it with the site’s own P01–P08 average (no donors).
C3 (ours) — prediction error < error of the equal-weight (1/5 each) donor average at the same period: does the optimization earn anything?

Reading

C2 is the S1 failure: at P10 only 4 of the sites beat their own history in any configuration. C3’s 7–9 passes only rank donors against each other — with C2 failing, neither helps.

5 · Analysis — why it fails where it fails

Reading the failures through the FSC error bound

Measurement noise

The FSC paper’s finite-sample bound assumes each observed outcome = true outcome + mean-zero noise of scale σ, and grows with σ(1 + ‖ŵ‖2). We do not compute the bound; it structures an interpretation: contaminated pixels change the computed statistics while the ground does not, which acts as noise of larger σ. Generation fill is worse than noise: it can shift the outcome distribution (a bias, violating the mean-zero assumption). Consistent with the measured P09 failures in both pipelines.

Pre-treatment approximation

The bound depends directly on the pre-fit term

 T0Σt=1 ‖Ψ(ν1t) − Σjŵj Ψ(νjt)‖ℋ2 1/2

A poor pre-treatment fit weakens the guarantee even when the outcome geometry is correctly specified — S1 fails here.

  • M T0 is not the count of independent constraints: fertility curves and quantile functions are smooth, so effective dimension ≪ M — how mortality fits a 100-point function with 17 donors.
  • Larger M: more within-period information, plus redundancy and noise. More T0: more evidence that donor relations are stable (and harder exact matching) — no extra weights. More J: larger convex hull, but with few fit periods many combinations fit alike and weights destabilize.
6 · Conclusion and next steps

What we learned, what remains open

Takeaways

  1. The 980-d failure was a representation failure, not an estimator failure. Pooling roughly halves the raw-latent error (S1 1.08 → 0.60, S2 1.11 → 0.58 standardized RMSE); switching estimators within a family changes almost nothing.
  2. FSC contributes the representation framework, not estimation gains: barycenters / PSD averages make the counterfactual well-defined. Its weight optimization — like ridge-ASCM and text-SC ridge — adds ≈ nothing.
  3. Donors and pre-treatment history are the binding constraint, especially the donors: fitted weights add ≈ nothing over a plain donor average (C3 ≈ coin flip), and on Sentinel-1 latents the donors add nothing over the site’s own history (C2). Three estimator families tie, so the fix is a richer donor pool or representation, not a better estimator.
  4. Generation cloud fill is task-dependent — helps image-space work, damages the pooled fit. Sentinel-1 carries no donor signal in any latent representation.

Solved by this work

  • Why latent-space SC failed, measured — and what fixes it (pooling).
  • What the FSC-vs-SCM comparison captures; a reusable null-testing harness (the three comparisons C1–C3).
  • Direct comparability with the collaborator’s tables, on their own metric.

Unsolved / next

  • Cloud handling is now measured from both directions (generation fill, and no-fill masked pooling — the cloud slide): generation fill hurts, masking is ≈ cost-free but no rescue, dropping fabricated chips costs fit data. Remaining: validate on clean pre-periods only; quality-weighted period selection.
  • The FSC conformal prediction intervals are not yet built.
  • The heterogeneity claim (dispersion advantage of quantile/Gram) needs a dispersion-valued outcome experiment.
  • Sentinel-1: drop, or rethink its representation.
