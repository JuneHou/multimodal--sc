## Everything is yours to decide

`spec.json` holds four choices, the paper's options for building the synthetic control (Abadie, Diamond

and Hainmueller 2010, p. 496). … Every fit uses all 29 donors, and the Synth R package fits it with its

default optimizers and solver settings.

- `predictors`, `every_outcome` (Z1, Z2 and the outcome in each pre-treatment period, …) or `summaries`

  (Z1, Z2 and "only a few linear combination of preintervention outcomes", …). …

- `v`, how the predictor weights V are chosen. `judgement` sets V from "subjective assessments of the

  predictive power of the variables" and carries `values`, one weight per predictor. `pre_period_fit` …

  `validation` fits the donor weights on "an initial training period" ending at `training_end`, …

- `weights`, `convex` (every donor weight at least 0, summing to one, …) or `extrapolation` (summing to one,

  any weight may be below 0 or above one, "at the cost of allowing extrapolation").

- `penalty`, `none` or `distance`, "penalty terms specified as increasing functions of the distances" …

3. Write `spec.json`, then run `Rscript scripts/fit.R spec.json`. It prints the predictors and V, …

At σ = 1 three of the four scm‑design answers took the outcome in every pre‑treatment period as predictors, with V fitted to the pre‑treatment outcome. GLM‑5.3 on DGP 2 took three outcome summaries, with V chosen on a validation stretch. All four chose convex weights and no penalty.
